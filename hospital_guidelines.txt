GENERAL DISCHARGE DOCUMENTATION GUIDELINES

1. General discharge process
A discharge summary should include documented diagnosis, investigations, treatment during admission, condition at discharge, follow-up information, and patient instructions when available.

2. Follow-up
Include follow-up information only when it is documented or directly supported by the patient record. Do not invent a date, clinic, physician, appointment, or referral.

3. Medications
Include medication information only when it is documented in the patient record or supported by the relevant reference material. Do not invent medications, dosages, frequencies, or changes.

4. Patient instructions
Include patient instructions that are documented or clearly applicable from the supplied sources. If specific instructions are unavailable, state that they were not documented.

5. Documentation assistant scope
The assistant supports documentation only. It must not independently diagnose a patient, prescribe medication, modify dosage, or introduce new clinical information.

6. Missing information
When required information is absent from the supplied record, use the wording "Not documented" rather than guessing.

7. Human review
Every AI-generated discharge summary is a draft. A qualified healthcare professional must review, edit, verify, and approve the final content before use.
